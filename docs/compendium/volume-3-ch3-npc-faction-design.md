# I. NPC PHILOSOPHY

NPCs are not quest dispensers.

They are people.

Every meaningful NPC should appear to have existed before the players arrived and continue existing after the players leave.

The world should never feel like it only exists when the party is present.

NPCs should:

• Have goals
• Hold opinions
• Make mistakes
• Change over time
• Form relationships
• React to consequences

An NPC that only delivers exposition is a tool.

An NPC that pursues their own agenda becomes part of the world.

---

# II. THE ANATOMY OF AN NPC

Every major NPC can be designed with eight core attributes.

## 1. Role

What function do they serve?

Examples:

• Ally
• Mentor
• Merchant
• Noble
• Villain
• Scholar
• Guide
• Rival
• Priest
• Informant

Role defines why they appear.

## 2. Goal

What do they want?

Goals should be concrete.

Weak: "I want power."

Strong: "I want to become High Chancellor before winter."

## 3. Fear

Fear limits behavior.

Examples:

• Exposure
• Failure
• Poverty
• Betrayal
• Death
• Losing family
• Losing reputation
• Losing faith

Fear creates believable hesitation.

## 4. Resources

Resources determine influence.

Examples:

• Wealth
• Soldiers
• Knowledge
• Political office
• Magical ability
• Religious authority
• Criminal network

## 5. Flaw

Flaws create humanity.

Examples:

• Pride
• Cowardice
• Addiction
• Obsession
• Impatience
• Distrust
• Arrogance
• Mercy

Flaws should create future complications.

## 6. Secret

Secrets generate stories.

Examples:

• Hidden identity
• Illegitimate heir
• Secret cult member
• Magical curse
• Betrayal
• Hidden crime
• Forbidden love

Secrets should matter if discovered.

## 7. Relationship

How does the NPC connect to others?

Family

Friends

Enemies

Political allies

Former mentors

Students

Rivals

Debtors

Creditors

Relationships produce stories automatically.

## 8. Voice

Voice includes:

Vocabulary

Speech rhythm

Confidence

Humor

Formality

Accent (optional)

Catchphrases (rarely)

Speech should communicate personality, not become parody.

# III. NPC ARCHETYPES

**MENTOR**

Purpose

Teach.

Challenge.

Eventually step aside.

Examples

Wizard

Veteran knight

Retired thief

Old ranger

**RIVAL**

Purpose

Push players to improve.

Not necessarily evil.

Examples

Another adventuring party.

Military officer.

Academic rival.

Treasure hunter.

**PATRON**

Purpose

Provides missions.

Resources.

Political support.

Should possess independent priorities.

**ALLY**

Reliable.

Not always available.

Sometimes wrong.

May require rescue.

**MERCHANT**

Represents economy.

Can also distribute rumors, information, and political insight.

**SCHOLAR**

Provides context.

Not solutions.

**GUIDE**

Knows terrain.

Often lacks combat ability.

Should possess local knowledge unavailable elsewhere.

**FOIL**

Represents an alternative worldview.

Challenges player assumptions.

Not necessarily hostile.

# IV. MEMORABLE NPC DESIGN

Players remember differences.

Not biographies.

Give each important NPC:

One visual trait.

One behavioral trait.

One conversational trait.

One hidden trait.

**Example**

Captain Arlen

Visual — Missing left hand.

Behavior — Always stands.

Conversation — Never wastes words.

Hidden — Cannot read.

Simple.

Memorable.

Believable.

# V. DIALOGUE SYSTEMS

NPC dialogue should reveal:

Information

Emotion

Goals

Relationship

Conflict

History

Avoid exposition dumps.

Instead:

Answer questions imperfectly.

Interrupt.

Ask questions.

Lie.

Deflect.

Misremember.

Reveal bias.

People rarely explain themselves objectively.

---

# VI. MOTIVATION ENGINE

Most motivations fall into six categories.

Power

Security

Love

Knowledge

Freedom

Purpose

Every major NPC should prioritize one.

Conflicts occur when priorities collide.

**Example**

```
General  → Security
    ↓
Merchant → Freedom
    ↓
King     → Power
    ↓
Priest   → Purpose
    ↓
Scholar  → Knowledge
```

The story writes itself.

---

# VII. RELATIONSHIP WEBS

Think in networks.

Not isolated NPCs.

**Example**

```
King
│
├── General
│
├── Queen
│
├── Spymaster
│
└── Chancellor
```

Each relationship has:

Trust

Fear

Debt

Respect

Love

Hatred

Envy

Loyalty

Changing one relationship should influence others.

If the General dies...

The Queen loses support.

The Chancellor gains influence.

The Spymaster becomes vulnerable.

Politics evolves naturally.

# VIII. VILLAIN DESIGN

Great villains believe they are justified.

They should answer four questions.

## 1. What do they want?

Examples:

• End war
• Achieve immortality
• Restore empire
• Protect family
• Free imprisoned god

## 2. Why do they believe they are right?

The answer should be understandable, even if morally unacceptable.

Example:

"The kingdom only survives through fear."

## 3. Why can't they stop?

Examples:

• Pride
• Prophecy
• Addiction
• Duty
• Revenge
• Political momentum
• Magical corruption

## 4. What would make them surrender?

Sometimes: Nothing.

Sometimes: Proof.

Sometimes: Mercy.

Sometimes: Death.

A villain without limits feels artificial.

---

# IX. FACTION PHILOSOPHY

Factions make the world move.

Players should feel like events continue even when they leave.

A faction should pursue its goals whether or not the party exists.

Players influence the world.

They are not the engine of it.

Every faction answers:

What do we want?

What are we willing to do?

What won't we do?

Who opposes us?

Who secretly supports us?

What happens if no one interferes?

# X. FACTION CONSTRUCTION

Every faction should define:

Name

Mission

Leader

Resources

Territory

Membership

Recruitment

Enemies

Allies

Ideology

Methods

Current Problem

Future Goal

**Example**

**The Ash Brotherhood**

Mission — Destroy magical aristocracy.

Leader — Former royal battlemage.

Resources — Hidden fortresses, Veterans, Smugglers

Ideology — Magic belongs to everyone.

Methods — Sabotage. Propaganda. Selective assassination.

Current Problem — Running out of safe houses.

Future Goal — Start civil war.

## XI. Governments *(bridge)*

Canonical sources: [Vol II Part VIII §6–9](volume-2-design.md) (political axes,
factions, moral claim, political conflict); [Vol II Part VI §4–6](volume-2-design.md)
(nation design formula, two-trait rule, national identity).

Working synthesis: model a government on four axes rather than a type label —
**authority** (centralized ↔ decentralized), **tradition** (conservative ↔
reformist), **legitimacy** (sacred / popular / hereditary / military /
economic), **method** (consensus ↔ coercion). Give every government one
defining strength and one internal contradiction, and let its response to
player action follow from its legitimacy source: a sacred monarchy answers
heresy, a merchant council answers disruption of trade.

## XII. Reputation Systems *(bridge)*

Canonical source: [Vol III Ch9 §IX](volume-3-ch9-advanced-dm-systems.md)
(Reputation & Renown) — the −5 Hated … +5 Legendary scale, tracked separately
per faction, with benefits emerging naturally (discounts, access, titles,
information).

NPC-level extension consistent with this chapter's §VII: individual NPCs track
on *axes* (trust, fear, respect, affection, debt) rather than one scalar — an
NPC can trust you and fear you at once. Lantern implements exactly this as
`npc_disposition` ledger entries.

## XIII. Living World Simulation *(bridge)*

Canonical sources: [Vol III Ch9 §VI](volume-3-ch9-advanced-dm-systems.md)
(Faction Turns), [Ch9 §I](volume-3-ch9-advanced-dm-systems.md) (the world moves
without the heroes), [Vol II Part XIV §3–5](volume-2-design.md) (institutional
response, consequence tracking, the world-state log), and this chapter's §IX
(factions pursue goals whether or not the party exists).

Working synthesis: between adventures each major faction takes one strategic
action resolved by goal and resources; institutions respond to major events
(who profits, who panics); track only meaningful changes. Lantern implements
the boundary-advance as `faction_clock` entries whose fills visibly re-gate
content.

## XIV. NPC Audit *(bridge)*

Assembled in the audit style of every other chapter, from this chapter's own
§I–VIII requirements:

- [ ] The NPC has a concrete goal, not an abstraction.
- [ ] A fear limits their behavior.
- [ ] Their resources explain their influence.
- [ ] A flaw creates future complications.
- [ ] Their secret matters if discovered.
- [ ] They connect to at least two other NPCs or factions.
- [ ] Their voice is distinguishable without a nametag.
- [ ] They would keep pursuing their goal if the party vanished.
- [ ] Their dialogue reveals imperfectly — deflection, bias, omission.
- [ ] If they are a villain: the four villain questions (§VIII) have answers.
