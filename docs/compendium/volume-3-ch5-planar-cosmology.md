# I. COSMOLOGICAL PHILOSOPHY

A cosmology answers the largest questions in a setting.

• Where did reality come from?
• What happens after death?
• Where do gods reside?
• Where does magic originate?
• Are souls eternal?
• Can reality itself change?
• What lies beyond the known universe?

A campaign can ignore these questions for many sessions, but the setting benefits from having consistent answers.

---

# II. THE STRUCTURE OF REALITY

Reality is often modeled in layers.

**FOUNDATION**

The fundamental laws that govern existence.

Examples:

• Time
• Space
• Souls
• Magic
• Matter
• Thought

**PLANES**

Distinct realms with their own physical or metaphysical rules.

**BOUNDARIES**

The barriers separating realities.

**PATHWAYS**

Methods of crossing between planes.

**ANCHORS**

Locations or objects that stabilize reality.

---

# III. COSMOLOGICAL MODELS

## 1. GREAT WHEEL

The classic D&D arrangement.

Features:

• Material Plane
• Ethereal Plane
• Feywild
• Shadowfell
• Inner Planes
• Outer Planes
• Astral Plane

Strengths:

• Familiar
• Extensive lore
• Moral alignment structure

Limitations:

• Can feel rigid if alignment dominates cosmology.

## 2. WORLD TREE

Planes branch from a central world.

Travel resembles movement through living roots and branches.

Strong for mythic campaigns.

## 3. WORLD AXIS

Reality organized around opposing cosmic forces.

Examples:

• Civilization vs Chaos
• Life vs Death
• Creation vs Entropy

Supports clear thematic conflict.

## 4. LAYERED COSMOS

Reality resembles stacked dimensions.

Examples:

```
Surface World
    ↓
Spirit World
    ↓
Dream Realm
    ↓
Underworld
    ↓
Primordial Void
```

Simple and intuitive.

## 5. FRACTAL COSMOS

Every world contains gateways to infinitely repeating realities.

Useful for:

• High-concept fantasy
• Time loops
• Reality manipulation
• Infinite exploration

---

# IV. THE MATERIAL PLANE

The Material Plane serves as the campaign's primary reality.

Characteristics:

• Stable physical laws
• Mortal civilizations
• Limited divine intervention
• Resource scarcity
• Historical continuity

Questions:

• Is the Material Plane central to creation?
• One world or many?
• Are there hidden continents?
• Is the universe finite?
• Do stars represent suns, gods, or portals?

---

# V. ECHO PLANES

Echo planes mirror the Material while expressing specific metaphysical themes.

**FEYWILD**

Represents:

• Emotion
• Growth
• Wonder
• Excess
• Ancient nature

Characteristics:

• Time distortion
• Powerful emotions
• Living forests
• Impossible beauty
• Fey courts

**SHADOWFELL**

Represents:

• Loss
• Decay
• Memory
• Entropy
• Despair

Characteristics:

• Ruins
• Mists
• Undead
• Faded color
• Emotional numbness

Echo planes should not merely copy geography.

They reinterpret it.

---

# VI. ELEMENTAL REALMS

Elemental planes embody fundamental forces.

**AIR** — Motion, Freedom, Storms, Height

**EARTH** — Endurance, Wealth, Stability

**FIRE** — Passion, Destruction, Transformation

**WATER** — Adaptation, Memory, Change

Additional possibilities:

• Lightning
• Ice
• Metal
• Wood
• Void
• Light
• Sound
• Crystal

Elemental realms should feel culturally distinct rather than simply environmentally different.

---

# VII. OUTER PLANES

Outer Planes often represent ideals rather than geography.

Possible organizing principles:

• Alignment
• Philosophy
• Emotion
• Virtue
• Sin
• Cosmic purpose
• Divine domains

Examples:

Realm of Justice

Realm of War

Realm of Dreams

Realm of Knowledge

Realm of Endless Battle

Realm of Perfect Order

Realm of Infinite Freedom

Travel to an Outer Plane should challenge beliefs as much as survival.

---

# VIII. DIVINE REALMS

Questions to answer:

• Where do gods dwell?
• Can mortals visit?
• Can gods die?
• Do divine realms change?
• Can realms be conquered?
• Do multiple gods share domains?

Possible structures:

• Individual divine kingdoms
• Shared celestial city
• Living constellation
• Floating mountain
• Endless library
• Infinite battlefield
• Sea of souls

---

# IX. THE AFTERLIFE

Death shapes culture.

Possible models:

**JUDGMENT** — Souls are evaluated.

**REINCARNATION** — Souls return.

**ASCENSION** — Great souls become divine.

**DISSOLUTION** — Souls merge with creation.

**ANCESTRAL** — Souls remain with descendants.

**MULTIPLE DESTINATIONS** — Different beliefs or actions determine different outcomes.

Questions:

Can resurrection interrupt judgment?

Can souls refuse the afterlife?

Can souls be stolen?

Can gods compete for souls?

Consequences

# XI. PLANAR HAZARDS

Examples:

• Temporal distortion
• Gravity inversion
• Identity erosion
• Memory loss
• Elemental exposure
• Soul instability
• Language alteration
• Magical saturation
• Conceptual paradox

Hazards should reinforce the plane's theme.

---

# XII. PLANAR CIVILIZATIONS

Intelligent planar societies require:

• Government
• Economy
• Religion
• Conflict
• Culture
• Resources
• Architecture
• Relationships with other planes

Avoid making planes feel empty except for monsters.

---

# XIII. COSMOLOGICAL CONFLICTS

Examples:

• Gods at war
• Elemental imbalance
• Planes colliding
• Reality fracturing
• Dream invading reality
• Dead plane awakening
• Time unraveling
• Soul famine
• Astral plague

Large-scale campaigns often emerge from cosmological instability.

---

# XIV. REALITY-ALTERING EVENTS

Examples:

• Birth of a new god
• Death of a plane
• Collapse of the afterlife
• Merger of realities
• Time fracture
• Primordial awakening
• Permanent portal opening
• Loss of magic
• Rewriting history

Such events should permanently alter the setting.

---

# XV. BUILDING AN ORIGINAL COSMOLOGY

Answer the following:

1. What existed first?
2. How was reality created?
3. Where does magic originate?
4. Where do souls come from?
5. What happens after death?
6. Why do gods exist?
7. What lies outside reality?
8. Can reality end?
9. What threatens creation?
10. Why have ordinary people survived until now?

These answers become the metaphysical foundation of the campaign.

---

# XVI. COSMOLOGY AUDIT

- [ ] Creation myth is internally consistent.
- [ ] Origin of magic is defined.
- [ ] Afterlife has understandable rules.
- [ ] Planes have distinct identities.
- [ ] Travel has meaningful cost.
- [ ] Divine realms influence the world.
- [ ] Cosmological conflicts create adventure.
- [ ] Reality follows consistent metaphysical laws.
- [ ] Players can eventually learn how the universe works.
- [ ] Cosmology reinforces campaign themes.

# FLINT'S COSMOLOGY GOLDEN RULE

Players should eventually realize that the universe has rules.

Those rules may be mysterious.

They may be ancient.

They may even be wrong.

But they should never be arbitrary.

The larger the universe becomes, the more important internal consistency becomes.
