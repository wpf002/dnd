# I. CAMPAIGN PHILOSOPHY

A campaign is not a novel.

It is not a screenplay.

It is not a video game.

A campaign is an evolving collaboration between:

• Dungeon Master
• Players
• Dice
• World
• Consequences

The DM creates situations.

Players create decisions.

The dice determine uncertainty.

The campaign emerges from their interaction.

The objective is not to tell your story.

The objective is to create a world where remarkable stories naturally occur.

---

# II. THE THREE LEVELS OF STORY

Every successful campaign operates simultaneously on three levels.

## 1. Session Story

A single evening's adventure.

Examples:

• Escape the prison.
• Negotiate with the duke.
• Clear the mine.
• Survive the ambush.

Beginning

Middle

End

## 2. Arc Story

Several connected sessions.

Examples:

• Civil war
• Dragon hunt
• Political election
• Ancient curse

Usually spans multiple levels.

## 3. Campaign Story

The largest narrative.

Usually begins with a premise.

Ends with transformation.

Examples:

Restore the shattered empire.

Prevent the return of an elder god.

Reunite the broken kingdoms.

Defeat the immortal tyrant.

The campaign story should remain visible without dominating every session.

---

# III. CAMPAIGN PREMISE

A campaign premise answers three questions.

Where?

Who?

Why now?

**Example**

The kingdoms of the west are collapsing after the death of the last dragon, and dozens of factions race to claim the ancient Dragon Crown.

Immediately:

Location.

Conflict.

Urgency.

Potential stories.

Players understand why adventures matter.

# V. STORY ARCS

Every arc should contain five phases.

**Phase 1 — Introduction**

Players discover the problem.

**Phase 2 — Expansion**

Complications appear.

Multiple factions emerge.

**Phase 3 — Escalation**

The antagonist gains momentum.

Players suffer setbacks.

**Phase 4 — Revelation**

Important truths emerge.

Motivations change.

History is reinterpreted.

**Phase 5 — Resolution**

Conflict concludes.

Consequences reshape the world.

Then the next arc begins.

Campaigns feel alive when arcs overlap slightly.

# VI. SESSION ARCHITECTURE

Recommended session rhythm

```
Opening
    ↓
Exploration
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Conflict
    ↓
Discovery
    ↓
Resolution
    ↓
New Question
```

Every session should end by creating curiosity for the next.

Players should leave asking:

"What happens now?"

rather than

"Are we done?"

# VIII. PLAYER AGENCY

Agency means choices matter.

The DM controls:

World.

NPCs.

Consequences.

History.

Players control:

Goals.

Methods.

Relationships.

Risk.

The DM should never invalidate meaningful player decisions simply because they disrupt prepared material.

Instead:

Adapt the world.

Not the players.

---

# IX. FORESHADOWING

Foreshadowing rewards attention.

Methods:

Recurring symbols

Rumors

Dreams

Songs

Ancient murals

Historical records

Small unexplained events

Repeated NPC warnings

A good revelation should make players think:

"We should have seen that."

Not:

"How could we possibly know?"

---

# X. MYSTERIES

Mysteries require three things.

Question

Evidence

Answer

Never create mysteries without answers.

Players should discover truth.

Not wait for exposition.

Provide multiple clues.

One failed Investigation check should never halt the campaign.

If one clue is missed...

Another exists elsewhere.

---

# XI. VILLAIN ENGINEERING

Villains act.

They do not wait.

Every major villain possesses:

Goal

Plan

Resources

Timeline

Contingencies

Weakness

If the party ignores them...

The villain should still progress.

Players interrupt plans.

They do not create them.

---

# XII. STORY TWISTS

A twist should:

Reveal hidden truth.

Not replace logic.

**Weak twist**

"It was all a dream."

**Strong twist**

"The kingdom you've protected was secretly built atop the prison you've been trying to keep sealed."

Good twists reinterpret previous events.

They don't erase them.

---

# XIII. CAMPAIGN ENDINGS

A satisfying ending answers:

Did the heroes change?

Did the world change?

Did their choices matter?

Victory should never return the world to its exact previous state.

History should move forward.

---

# XIV. STORY AUDIT

- [ ] Clear campaign premise.
- [ ] Strong central conflict.
- [ ] Multiple factions.
- [ ] Active villain.
- [ ] Escalating stakes.
- [ ] Player agency preserved.
- [ ] Foreshadowing present.
- [ ] Multiple mysteries.
- [ ] Meaningful consequences.
- [ ] Ending changes the setting.

---

# DAVIS' STORY GOLDEN RULE

Never ask,

"What story do I want to tell?"

Instead ask,

**"What story would naturally emerge if these people pursued these goals in this world?"**

That single question separates a living campaign from a scripted adventure.
