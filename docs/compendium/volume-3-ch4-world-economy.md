# I. ECONOMIC DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

## 1. ECONOMY CREATES CONFLICT

An economy determines:

• Who has access to food
• Who controls weapons
• Who owns land
• Who travels safely
• Who receives education
• Who can afford magic
• Who pays for war
• Who profits from instability

Economic pressure creates stories naturally.

A kingdom does not invade merely because it is evil.

It may invade because:

• Its harvest failed
• Its mines are exhausted
• Its trade route was blocked
• Its currency is collapsing
• Its population is growing
• Its rulers need external enemies

## 2. ECONOMIC REALISM VERSUS PLAYABILITY

A fantasy economy does not need to simulate every transaction.

It must explain the forces that matter to play.

Focus on:

• Scarcity
• Control
• Movement
• Access
• Risk
• Political consequence

Ignore details that produce bookkeeping without meaningful decisions.

## 3. VALUE IS CONTEXTUAL

The value of an item depends on:

• Location
• Scarcity
• Demand
• Law
• Season
• War
• Religion
• Transportation cost
• Magical utility

A barrel of salt may be ordinary in a coastal city and priceless in an isolated mountain fortress.

## 4. ECONOMY AS WORLD-BUILDING

Economic design should answer:

• Why does this city exist?
• What does it produce?
• What must it import?
• Who controls trade?
• Who performs labor?
• What happens when supply fails?
• Who becomes powerful because of this system?

# V. PRICES AND PURCHASING POWER

## 1. PRICE RELATIVITY

A price list is only a baseline.

Prices change based on:

• Local supply
• Demand
• War
• Season
• Distance
• Law
• Reputation
• Urgency

## 2. PRICE TIERS

**COMMON** — Ordinary food, clothing, tools, lodging

**SPECIALIZED** — Weapons, armor, skilled services, rare books

**LUXURY** — Jewelry, fine art, exotic food, noble clothing

**MAGICAL** — Potions, scrolls, enchanted items, resurrection

**STRATEGIC** — Siege engines, ships, warhorses, teleportation access

## 3. REGIONAL MODIFIERS

**SURPLUS REGION** — Prices fall. Example: Grain in fertile valley.

**SCARCITY REGION** — Prices rise. Example: Water in desert city.

**SIEGE** — Food and medicine rise sharply.

**POSTWAR** — Weapons may become cheap while labor becomes scarce.

**PILGRIMAGE SEASON** — Lodging and religious goods become expensive.

## 4. BARGAINING

Bargaining may depend on:

• Culture
• Market type
• Reputation
• Urgency
• Quantity
• Relationship

Do not require repeated bargaining rolls for routine purchases.

Use negotiation when the outcome matters.

# XIX. RESOURCE-TRACKING MECHANICS

## 1. ABSTRACT RESOURCE SCORES

Track resources from 0 to 5.

```
0 — None
1 — Scarce
2 — Limited
3 — Stable
4 — Abundant
5 — Dominant
```

Possible categories:

• Food
• Wealth
• Military supply
• Magical resources
• Public trust

## 2. RESOURCE DICE

Assign a die:

d4, d6, d8, d10, d12

When heavily used, roll the die.

On a low result, reduce the die size.

This models depletion without exact counting.

## 3. FACTION ECONOMIC POWER

Rate factions in:

• Wealth
• Labor
• Trade
• Land
• Magic
• Military supply

Use scores from 1 to 10.

## 4. PRICE SHOCKS

Temporary price changes may be represented as:

**Minor:** ±10%

**Moderate:** ±25%

**Severe:** ±50%

**Crisis:** Double or unavailable
