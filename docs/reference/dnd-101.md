# Dungeons & Dragons 101 — Beginner's Foundation

> **App role.** This is the source for in-app onboarding, rules tooltips, and
> the glossary. Where the compendium volumes teach *design*, this teaches
> *play* — it is the only document in the corpus written for someone who has
> never rolled a d20. Every term defined here should resolve to a tooltip in
> `apps/web`.
>
> §15 (The Golden Rule) is the specification for Lantern's free-text input
> field: the player types what their character attempts, and the engine — not
> the player — decides which roll applies.

## 1. WHAT IS D&D?

Dungeons & Dragons (D&D) is a tabletop role-playing game (TTRPG) where players create fictional heroes and work together to tell an adventure guided by a Dungeon Master (DM).

Unlike a board game, there is no predetermined path. Players can attempt nearly anything, and the DM determines what happens.

**Core Gameplay Loop:**

1. DM describes the situation.
2. Players describe what they want to do.
3. Dice determine uncertain outcomes.
4. DM narrates the results.
5. Repeat.

The goal is collaborative storytelling—not "winning."

• Creates adventures
• Interprets the rules
• Decides when dice are rolled

### Players

• Create a unique character
• Make decisions
• Roleplay
• Fight monsters
• Solve problems
• Shape the story

## 3. THE DICE

```
d4   = Four-sided
d6   = Six-sided
d8   = Eight-sided
d10  = Ten-sided
d12  = Twelve-sided
d20  = Twenty-sided (most important die)
d100 = Percentile (usually two d10s)
```

The d20 is used for:

• Attacks
• Skill checks
• Saving throws

Examples:

• Grappling

### Dexterity (DEX)

Examples:

Knowledge and reasoning.

• History
• Investigation
• Nature

### Wisdom (WIS)

Awareness and intuition.

Examples:
• Perception
• Insight
• Survival
• Medicine

### Charisma (CHA)

Presence and personality.

Examples:
• Persuasion
• Deception
• Performance
• Intimidation

## 5. THE THREE MOST COMMON ROLLS

### Ability Check

"Can I do this?"

Examples:
• Climb a wall
• Convince a guard
• Recall magical knowledge

Formula:

```
d20 + Ability Modifier + Proficiency (if applicable)
```

### Attack Roll

"Can I hit the target?"

Formula:

```
d20 + Attack Bonus
```

Must equal or exceed the target's Armor Class (AC).

### Saving Throw

"Can I resist something happening to me?"

Examples:
• Dodge a fireball
• Resist poison
• Ignore magical fear

---

## 6. SKILL CHECKS

Skills are specialized uses of abilities.

**Arcana (INT)** — Knowledge of magic, spells, magical creatures, enchantments, runes

**History (INT)** — Knowledge of kingdoms, wars, historical figures

**Nature (INT)** — Knowledge of plants, animals, terrain

**Religion (INT)** — Knowledge of gods, undead, divine traditions

**Perception (WIS)** — Notice things: hidden enemies, traps, secret doors

**Insight (WIS)** — Read people: Is someone lying? Hidden motives

**Survival (WIS)** — Living in the wilderness: tracking, navigation, hunting

**Persuasion (CHA)** — Convincing others

**Stealth (DEX)** — Remaining unseen

**Athletics (STR)** — Running, jumping, swimming, climbing

---

## 7. PASSIVE PERCEPTION

Passive Perception represents what your character notices without actively searching.

Formula:

```
10 + Wisdom Modifier + Proficiency (if proficient)
```

Example:

Passive Perception = 15

If a goblin rolls a 13 on Stealth, you automatically notice it.

No dice roll required.

## 9. WHAT ARE FEATURES?

Features are special abilities your character gains from:

• Class
• Race
• Background
• Subclass

Examples:

**Fighter**

Second Wind — Heal yourself once per short or long rest.

Action Surge — Gain one additional action on your turn.

**Rogue**

Sneak Attack — Extra damage under the right conditions.

**Barbarian**

Rage — Increased damage and resistance.

**Paladin**

Divine Smite — Spend spell slots for radiant burst damage.

---

## 10. COMBAT TURN

On your turn you usually have:

### Movement

Move up to your speed.

### One Action

Examples:

• Attack
• Cast a spell
• Dash
• Dodge
• Help
• Hide
• Search
• Use an object

### One Bonus Action

Only if something grants one.

Examples:

• Second Wind
• Off-hand attack
• Certain spells

### One Reaction

Used outside your turn.

Examples:

• Opportunity Attack
• Shield spell
• Counterspell

---

## 11. HIT POINTS

HP represents your ability to keep fighting.

At 0 HP:

• You fall unconscious.
• You begin making Death Saving Throws.

Three successes: Stable.

Three failures: Death.

---

## 12. RESTING

### Short Rest

About one hour.

Recovers abilities like:
• Second Wind
• Action Surge
• Some class resources

### Long Rest

About eight hours.

Recovers:
• Full HP (normally)
• Spell slots
• Most class features

---

## 13. LEVELING UP

As you gain experience (or when the DM decides), you level up.

Leveling grants:

• More HP
• Better abilities
• New Features
• Stronger spells
• Higher proficiency bonus
• Subclass abilities
• Extra attacks (certain classes)

---

## 14. ROLEPLAYING

You are not controlling a game piece.

You ARE the character.

Ask yourself:

• What would they say?
• What do they fear?
• What motivates them?
• Who do they trust?
• What are they willing to sacrifice?

---

## 15. THE GOLDEN RULE

When you don't know what to do:

Simply tell the DM what your character attempts.

Don't think in terms of game mechanics.

Instead of saying:

> "I roll Investigation."

Say:

> "I search the bookshelf for hidden compartments."

The DM will determine whether a roll is needed and which one.

## BEGINNER VOCABULARY

**AC (Armor Class)** — How hard you are to hit.

**HP** — Hit Points.

**DC** — Difficulty Class. The number you must meet or exceed to succeed.

**NPC** — Non-Player Character.

**Campaign** — A long adventure.

**One-Shot** — A single-session adventure.

**Party** — The player characters adventuring together.

**Initiative** — Determines combat turn order.

**Proficiency Bonus** — A bonus added to skills, weapons, and saves your character is trained in.

**Advantage** — Roll two d20s. Keep the higher.

**Disadvantage** — Roll two d20s. Keep the lower.

---

## BEGINNER PHILOSOPHY

There is almost never only one solution.

If you can imagine it, you can usually attempt it.

The dice determine success.
The DM determines consequences.
The players determine the story.

That freedom is what makes Dungeons & Dragons unique.
